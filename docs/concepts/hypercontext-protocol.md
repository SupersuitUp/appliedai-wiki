# Hypercontext Protocol

*The social contract for connecting a person's sovereign context to services and agents they don't own.*

---

The canonical, evolving reference lives at **[hypercontextprotocol.com](https://hypercontextprotocol.com)**. This page exists to place the term in this wiki's lexicon and route you there.

The one-paragraph version: people are accumulating deep personal context (preferences, projects, goals, relationships, expertise), and the services that could use it either surveil for it or never get it. The Hypercontext Protocol names the contract for the alternative: a person holds their context sovereignly, a curator agent working only for them passes a job-scoped sliver across the boundary, and the service honors minimal disclosure, purpose-bound use, transparent holdings, expiring grants, and real deletion on disconnect. "Connect your context" replaces "sign in with Google," and the discipline in one line is: share for relevance, never beyond excellence of service. Don't overshare.

Where MCP is the bridge between an agent and a service, the Hypercontext Protocol is the layer above it: what of a person's life may cross that bridge, on what terms, and for how long. The two compose; see [the canonical comparison](https://hypercontextprotocol.com/protocol/hypercontext-vs-mcp).

For the applied-AI engineer, the protocol matters as a design posture today, before any formal standard exists: products built to be excellent on a scoped sliver, with legible holdings and clean exits, are building toward where personalization is going. Products built to hoard context are building toward a backlash.
