# A Corpus Plus a Form Is Most of the Book

*When a body of life material exists as an annotated corpus, and a story form (the shape of a specific kind of book) has been specified as a buildable artifact, an agentic production process can carry a draft to 80 to 90 percent done. What remains for the human is detailed editing, taste, and truth-checking, which is a different job than writing.*

## Two inputs, one book

Consider a memoir like Michael Singer's *The Surrender Experiment*. Strip away the specific life it recounts and a definite shape remains: a chronological account told as a series of surrenders, where each beat pairs a decision to let go of personal preference with the improbable door that opened next, and the accumulation argues a single thesis about how life works. That shape is a **story form**, and it is separable from any particular life.

Now put two artifacts side by side:

- **A corpus.** The raw material of a documented life with provenance and annotation: biographical facts, dated events, the coincidences and their dependency chains, the people, the quotes, the turning points. This is the durable half described in [The Corpus and the Projection](/concepts/the-corpus-and-the-projection), built by the capture discipline in [The Capture-First Autobiography](/playbooks/capture-first-autobiography).
- **A form spec.** The story form written down as a buildable artifact: the spine, the beat structure, the refrain, the selection criteria for which corpus material qualifies as a beat, and the evaluation gates a candidate chapter must pass to count as an instance of the form.

Feed both to an agentic production process and the output is not notes toward a book. It is a book, most of the way done.

## Forms are specifiable, and that is the non-obvious part

The corpus half of this claim is now widely accepted. The form half is not, because a form spec is not obvious to build: nobody hands you the beat grammar of a published memoir, and extracting it takes real analytical work. But it is buildable, and the practice already exists in an adjacent medium. An [Agentic Brand Universe](/concepts/agentic-brand-universe) treats recurring kinds of work as **forms**: method files extracted from finished works, never speculated in advance, each stating its evidence base and its gates. A literary form is the same move applied to a book genre. One careful pass through *The Surrender Experiment* yields a reusable spec that any surrendered life with a good corpus can be poured into.

What makes this workable now is a capability that keeps surprising people who have not tested it: frontier models are strong at both **executing** a pattern and **evaluating** whether an output instantiates it. The same form spec that guides generation doubles as the rubric for [evals](/disciplines/evals) on the result, so the production process can reject its own off-form chapters before a human ever reads them. A form with gates is a [golden process](/concepts/golden-processes) for a whole genre, and each blessed chapter becomes a [golden example](/concepts/golden-examples) that sharpens the next run.

## The remaining 10 to 20 percent is a different job

An 80 to 90 percent done book is not a finished book, and the gap is not slack in the claim. The remaining work is the part that was always the human's: line-level taste, factual and emotional truth-checking against the life actually lived, the call on what stays private, and the judgment about whether a chapter that passes the form's gates also deserves to exist. That is editing, not drafting, and the economics of the two are entirely different. The blank page consumed years; the red pen takes weeks. This is the ordinary division of labor in agentic production: [The Human Role Splits Into Instigator and Editor](/perspectives/the-human-role-splits-into-instigator-and-editor), with the machine carrying the middle.

## What an operator should do with this

- **Capture beats drafting.** If a book is anywhere in someone's future, the rational move today is building the corpus, not writing chapters. The corpus compounds and never goes stale; drafts are cheap projections that get better every model generation. See [The Capture-First Autobiography](/playbooks/capture-first-autobiography).
- **Form specs are an asset class.** Every well-loved book genre is a latent spec waiting for one careful extraction, and a form, once built, amortizes across every corpus poured into it. This is [packaging the patterns you copy](/playbooks/package-the-patterns-you-copy) at the scale of a genre.
- **The moat stays where it was.** Anyone will be able to run the production process. The corpus (years of captured, annotated truth) and the blessed form library are the parts that cannot be conjured on demand.
