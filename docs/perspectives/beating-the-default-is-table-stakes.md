# Beating the Default Is Table Stakes

*Improving on the model's first pass only proves you are not worse than a machine working alone. The comparison that actually prices you is you plus AI against the median competitor plus AI.*

## The wrong benchmark is the comfortable one

The natural self-test is to take the model's first output and ask whether you can improve it. Most competent practitioners can, they feel reassured, and they stop there.

That test is set against the wrong opponent. The model working alone is not what you compete with, because **nobody in your field is going to be working without it**. Improving on a raw first pass establishes only that you are not worse than a machine by itself, which is a floor rather than an edge, and a floor almost everyone clears.

The comparison that decides what you get paid is **you plus AI against the median practitioner in your field plus AI.** That opponent is already better than the default, by roughly the amount an ordinary professional adds. Your delta has to exceed theirs, not exceed zero.

This is why the honest version of the question is uncomfortable in a way the comfortable version is not. Against the default, most people pass. Against the AI-assisted median, the distribution reasserts itself immediately, because everyone got the same lift.

## The measurement did not exist before

Expertise used to be assessed against other people, slowly, and mostly by reputation. There was no cheap instrument that could produce a competent baseline in your field on demand and let you compare your work to it in an afternoon.

There is now, and it changes what a practitioner can honestly know about themselves. The model's first pass is a mean: a defensible, unremarkable version of the thing, assembled from everything that has been done before. Your contribution is whatever you do to that pass. That quantity was always the real content of your expertise, and it was previously impossible to isolate because there was nothing to subtract.

## Three questions, answerable this week

**Do you have anything to add that corrects the default?** Take a first output in your own domain and try to make it materially better. Not restyled, not reformatted. Better in the way a client would notice and pay for. Treat a yes here as the floor, not the answer.

**Does your delta beat the median practitioner's delta?** This is the one people skip, and it is the question that decides the outcome. A practitioner can actively degrade an output by imposing preferences that are habits rather than expertise. More commonly they improve it by exactly as much as any competent peer would, which reads as success and prices as nothing. Barely better than what your AI-assisted competitor produces is the same answer as worse.

**Would your expertise survive being encoded?** If you worked with someone whose whole craft is turning expertise into systems, is there something in you that would make it into the system? If the answer is that your value is being present, responsive, and reliable, those are real virtues and they are not expertise. They will be priced as what they are.

## What a failing answer actually means

It is evidence about **fit**, and fit is more local than people assume.

The usual reading is that a bad result means leaving the field, and that is usually wrong. Most practitioners who fail these questions are badly placed inside the right field rather than in the wrong one, spending their hours on the portion of it that was never theirs. What the baseline exposes is which portion that is, and that is a more useful finding than a verdict on a career.

The practical response is to move toward the part of your work where your delta over an AI-assisted peer is largest, and to hand the rest to the systems that now do it at the baseline. That relocation is the whole job, and it is continuous rather than one-time.

Note what this rules out. Any advantage that consists of using AI at all has already expired, because the comparison assumes your competitor has it too. [Intelligence Is Commoditized, Deployment Is the Moat](/perspectives/intelligence-is-commoditized-deployment-is-the-moat) makes the same argument at the level of a company; this is the personal form of it.

## Why this got sharp suddenly

The uncomfortable version of the argument is that the difference between practitioners was previously **cloaked**. Output was bounded by a person's own hours, so a genuine gift and ordinary competence landed closer together than the difference between them warranted, and the compression read as normal.

That ceiling is lifting, and what surfaces first is not new capability. It is the gap that the ceiling was hiding.
