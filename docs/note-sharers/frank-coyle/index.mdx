# Frank Coyle

*A computer-science educator at UC Berkeley with 35 years in the field and an early career in neuroscience, who argues that agentic AI is bringing symbolic AI back and that the two halves belong wired together.*

---

## Worth tracking because

Coyle worked in expert systems during the 1980s boom and watched it collapse into the AI winter. That is an unusual vantage point in a field where most commentary starts in 2023. He is not nostalgic about it: the read is that the symbolic half failed on scale, the neural half has now solved scale, and the interesting engineering is at the join. For an operator, the value is the prior art. Formal knowledge representation has thirty years of mature vocabulary and tooling behind it, and most teams currently hand-rolling a validator around an agent are reinventing a worse version of it.

He teaches rather than sells, so the framings arrive without a product attached.

## Voice and stance

Discursive and historical. He reaches for lineage before technique: Aristotle before graph databases, Dartmouth 1956 before agents, the structured program theorem before agentic loops. The pedagogy is explicit and he states it up front, borrowed from Sister Corita Kent by way of John Cage: "Nothing is a mistake. There is no win. There's no fail. There's only make." He is blunt that hallucination is the mechanism rather than a defect, and that the fix is architectural rather than a better model.
