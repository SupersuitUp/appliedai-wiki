# Caleb Curry

*A solo software engineer and programming educator building a multi-tenant SaaS with Claude Code, who argues the breakthrough is converting written instructions into deterministic gates the agent cannot interpret its way past.*

---

## Worth tracking because

Caleb Curry has been making programming education content for over a decade, and he is now reporting from inside a real build: a multi-tenant platform for hosting products and running email marketing, developed over six months with Claude Code. The value for an operator is that he names the specific moment his results changed. He stopped adding rules to `CLAUDE.md` and started converting those rules into linting, types, database adapters, and hooks. Everything downstream in his setup follows from that one move.

He is specific about mechanism in a way most agentic-coding content is not. He gives the actual database adapter, the actual line-count limit, the actual container boundary, and the actual reason each one exists. He sells an agentic AI course and says so repeatedly, so read him as a practitioner with a product, and take the parts that survive contact with your own build.

## Voice and stance

Deliberately low on hype, and he announces that intent up front. He tells the story as a curve: early delight, then a collapse into frustration where he abandoned AI and hand-coded for a while, then a recovery once he understood where the agent belonged. That collapse is the load-bearing part of his credibility, because he can name what was actually failing.

His framing device is determinism. An LLM is valuable exactly where the same input may produce a different output, and it is a liability everywhere else. Most of his advice is an application of that one distinction to a different layer of the stack: rules, permissions, documentation, orchestration, and design.
