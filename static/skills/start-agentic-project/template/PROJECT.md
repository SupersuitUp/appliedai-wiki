## The deliverable

What is actually handed over at the end. Name the artifact, its format, and where
it goes. "A 20-page thesis on Bitcoin, PDF, submitted to the university portal."

Not "finish my thesis". A thing that can be pointed at.

## Milestones

**Every date in YYYY-MM-DD form, because `check.py` parses this file for them and
reports the nearest one as your real runway.**

The submission date is usually NOT the date that binds. If a reviewer needs a
draft two weeks earlier, or a decision has to be made before the research can
start, those are earlier and they are the ones that will actually catch you out.
Work backwards and list them all.

| Date | What is due | Derived from |
|---|---|---|
| YYYY-MM-DD | the final deliverable | the hard deadline |
| YYYY-MM-DD | draft to the reviewer | the deadline minus their lead time |
| YYYY-MM-DD | the decision that unblocks the work | how long the work after it takes |

Say for each whether it is real or self-imposed. The project manager treats those
differently.

## What good looks like

Specifics, not adjectives. What would a good version have that a mediocre one
would not? This is the section an agent uses to decide whether a draft is done.

## What would make this a failure

Even if delivered on time. This section is more useful than the one above it,
because it is easier to state and harder to argue with.

## Constraints

A university that penalises AI-assisted prose. A counterparty who reads
everything. A filing that needs a physical original.
